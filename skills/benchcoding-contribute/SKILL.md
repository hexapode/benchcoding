# Contribute an experiment

Read destination `AGENTS.md`, the collaboration guide, objective contract, [protocol](../benchcoding-create/references/protocol.md), and the selected [evaluation mode](../benchcoding-create/references/evaluation-modes.md). Find the owner, baseline manifest, permitted artifact types, evaluation stages, active hypotheses, and failed approaches. Resolve conflicting rules before spending the experiment budget.

1. Register a hypothesis with baseline identity, approach, expected benefit, team/payer, harness/model where used, related work, and bounded budget. Registration does not authorize funding: use an existing allocation or request one. Coordinate overlapping attempts through the documented route.
2. Start from the recorded baseline. Use an isolated checkout for code, a versioned workspace for designs/configurations/data/prompts, or the approved variant-preparation process for live work. Confirm access to development inputs and reproduction/review procedures. Do not expose users or deploy a variant without the required authorization.
3. Explore within the allowed artifact scope and budget. Record versions, procedures, failures, resource use, and findings. Do not include secrets or restricted participant data in public traces. Stop at the limit or owner redirection; retry within the allocation only.
4. Apply the permitted development evaluation. This may be local tests, a draft rubric review, or an offline check before a live trial. Record the environment, reviewers, and limitations as applicable. Treat observations as reported until independently verified. Do not weaken rules, inspect restricted answers, substitute proxy metrics for live outcomes, or treat missing human review as a pass. Report gaps to the owner with a concrete ambiguous case.
5. Submit an immutable candidate manifest and baseline, versions, hypothesis, evidence, costs, and reproduction/review instructions. Use full commits for code and fixed versions/digests for other artifacts; include relevant dependencies. A live proposal records the intended build/configuration and assignment plan, with exposure authorization separate. Changes after admission need a new candidate, not a mutable URL update.
6. Request evaluation through the documented route. The authorized dispatcher/reviewer reserves resources. Respect protected dispatch and review authority. When awaiting evidence or review, record the responsible party and next action; do not repeatedly query holdouts, prompt reviewers for a favorable answer, or extend a study past its stopping rule without an authorized follow-up.
7. Preserve findings-only and inconclusive experiments. Hand off the current artifact, evidence, remaining budget, and next question. Respond to review and reevaluate against changed baselines using the mode's procedure; historical live cohorts cannot simply be replayed. Acceptance and production changes remain the owner's decisions.

Finish with experiment/submission references, immutable artifact identity, reported evidence and its context, total known and unknown cost, current state, and pending next action. Never edit official evidence or the generated board to certify your own submission.
